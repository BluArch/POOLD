# POOLD organizer compensation and member savings

Research date: November 5, 2026. Currency: CAD. The supplied workbook was inspected read-only and remains unchanged. Keep this research uncommitted; current payment-plan revisions are identified below.

Pricing review update: this document preserves the earlier savings-based pricing research and its assumptions. The current recommendation is **store cost plus a transparent group service fee**, with proportional shared-cost allocation; it supersedes the half-savings allocation below. See [the payment plan](STRIPE_PAYMENTS_PLAN.md), section 3, and [the four-store catalogue plan](CATALOGUE_AND_PRICING_PLAN.md). The price observations remain historical evidence, not current local quotes.

## Finding

A $50 organizer reward can fit within grocery savings, but only when the final basket has enough verified price advantage and the group is sufficiently large. A fixed markup on every Costco item does not establish savings. Start with a curated catalogue and compare the full customer price against an available, acceptable local alternative.

Updated compensation decision: **one $50 organizer payment per completed run, with no separate car allowance**. This is in addition to grocery purchasing money. Travel expenses come out of the organizer's $50, so $50 is not guaranteed take-home income after their costs or personal taxes. This is particularly suitable for an organizer already planning the shopping trip; confirm willingness to do dedicated trips on these terms during the pilot.

Recommended pricing candidate: let the member retain at least half of the verified bulk saving, with an additional target of at least 10% below the comparison basket. Use the remaining margin for payment costs, organizer compensation and a modest operating allowance. Quote less than the pricing ceiling when the run still covers the target compensation and costs. Confirm commercial pricing/tax treatment before live launch. Do not describe the whole markup as a card-processing surcharge.

## Pricing rule to evaluate

For member i, let `C_i` be their fully allocated store cost and `R_i` an available, accepted retail comparison for the same quantity/grade. For goods with positive verified savings, start with:

```text
member price = C_i + 50% × (R_i − C_i)
maximum under the 10% savings target = 90% × R_i
allowed all-in price = smaller of those two amounts
```

Treat this as a ceiling, not permission to increase an accepted quote at closure. Do not apply the formula to goods with no savings and sell below cost accidentally. Mark them unavailable for the savings offer, or let the member knowingly choose a separately priced convenience/premium option without a savings claim.

On the observed sale examples, half the price gap gives approximately $12.35 for Kraft versus $16.71 equivalent retail, and $12.52 for Harvest Crunch versus $15.04 equivalent retail. Those prices are provisional planning figures, subject to local availability, product matching and any applicable tax/operating costs.

For a closed group, calculate total gross customer prices and subtract store cost, provider costs, operating allowance and the $50 organizer reward. Fund the reward only when the basket can support it while preserving the approved customer savings. A whole-group result must not hide one member paying above their comparison price. No separate transport charge or reimbursement is included.

This avoids promising a flat $50 for every group or arbitrarily increasing thin-margin items to subsidize other products. If there is insufficient margin at closure, offer a disclosed lower reward or cancel/revise before capture; never surprise-charge members. Any increased organizer reward or customer price needs the appropriate consent.

## Fee assumptions for the calculation

Planning scenario, not a provider quote:

- Card processing: 2.9% of captured customer total + $0.30 per successfully captured member payment, individually rounded.
- Connect payout: 0.25% + $0.25, plus Instant Payout at 1%, each percentage component rounded. Apply these to **goods + $50 organizer reward**, because that is the proposed bank payout.
- Account overhead: $2 allocation in this example, conservatively assuming this organizer has only one payout group in the active month. The underlying monthly charge is not per member or inherently per group; amortize across that organizer's actual monthly runs.
- Proposed operating allowance: $10/run. This is a planning budget, not evidence it covers all real operating costs or replaces funded refund capital.
- No platform profit target included in minimum-price calculations. Taxes on a separately supplied service, foreign-card extras, disputes, refund losses, additional routing charges and other expenses are excluded until their actual treatment/cost is established.

Sources: [Canadian card pricing](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing), [Canadian Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/en-ca/connect/pricing). Confirm contractual stacking, routing charges and treatment of manual authorizations before turning these assumptions into live prices.

For rough planning before cent-level allocation, with goods `C`, reward `W = $50`, paying members `n`, overhead `A`, operating allowance `B`, card rate `r`, card fixed fee `b`, combined payout percentage `q` and payout fixed fee `f`:

```text
organizer payout P = C + W
required customer gross X ≈ ((1 + q)P + f + n×b + A + B) / (1 − r)
required markup = X / C − 1
retail comparison needed for 10% member saving = X / 0.90
```

The actual calculation must allocate and round per member; the division is a planning approximation. Capturing five cards and making one payout does not eliminate the five card-processing fees.

## Five-member example that works conditionally

Assume each member's actual store basket costs $100 and a verified equivalent retail basket costs $150. This is an illustrative basket mix, **not a claim that the researched products prove a representative $100/$150 Langford basket**. The half-savings rule caps the price at $125. With the separate car allowance removed, a $120 final price (20% markup) covers this run's target and lets members retain $30 of the $50 price gap: a 20% saving against retail.

| Group cash allocation | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Five captured customer payments, $120 each | $600.00 |
| Goods | ($500.00) |
| Organizer reward | ($50.00) |
| Card costs, five × $3.78 | ($18.90) |
| Payout costs on $550.00 | ($7.13) |
| Account overhead budget | ($2.00) |
| Operating allowance | ($10.00) |
| Remaining contribution before excluded expenses/tax | **$11.97** |

Members collectively save $150 against the assumed $750 alternative. The single organizer payout is $550: $500 shopping money + $50 compensation, with no separate car allowance. The $11.97 contribution is not established net profit. If the same actual $500 goods cost only $600 at an acceptable alternative, a $600 checkout would not save members money and the offer must fail the savings check. Therefore 20% is an example markup that works for this basket and group size, not a universal catalogue rate.

Keeping the old $125-per-member price instead would leave $36.22 after the same $50 compensation, modeled provider costs, $2 overhead and $10 operating allowance. Recommend initially using that improvement to reduce customer prices and preserve a reasonable operating contribution, rather than automatically retaining the former price.

## Consequences for the payment plan, after pricing is agreed

Keep the user's timing rule: hold while open, finalize/capture only when the group closes, then one group Instant Payout after every required capture succeeds and funds qualify. Authorize the disclosed maximum **marked-up customer price**, including applicable taxes and fees, rather than the raw Costco cost.

At closure, use frozen price/fee policy, approved basket and retail references to calculate the actual final charge. Track goods, the $50 organizer compensation, provider expense and POOLD contribution separately. Include no transport reimbursement. If compensation is included in the initial payout, fees use goods plus compensation and cancellation/recovery rules must cover that compensation advance too. The $50 is payment for the run, not spendable grocery money; the organizer covers their travel within it.

Decide how discounts, shortfalls, store-price changes, cancellations and partial refunds affect the markup and reward before changing the Stripe plan. Maintain enough POOLD capital to refund after money is sent; the $10 allowance does not replace that reserve. Members must see their complete accepted price and meaningful savings comparison before authorizing.

Recommended pilot direction: curated, locally checked items; at least 10% savings per customer basket; members retain at least half of verified savings; one $50 organizer payment including their travel; and allow a run only when its actual margin covers the whole cost model. Aim initially for $500–$650 of funded goods, while testing demand, organizer willingness and car capacity, rather than treating that range as an automatic guarantee. Test whether the $50 terms attract repeat organizers before promising broader availability.
