# POOLD payments and fee implementation plan

Planning date: November 5, 2026. Revised November 6 to add buyer retries, group-wide notifications, voluntary share takeover and organizer cancellation after payment failure, and raise the organizer completion-reward minimum to $50. Pricing uses a 15% goods markup, a 5% service fee on the marked-up subtotal, and one organizer completion reward of **$50 or 10% of the paying members' original grocery cost, whichever is higher**. Grocery funding is paid before shopping; the reward is paid separately after completion. Current break-even calculations exclude operating costs and the monthly connected-account fee. Status: the November 6 demo and Stripe-sandbox implementation now uses the shared scheduled-payment workflow described in [the implementation guide](../implementation/PAYMENTS.md). Live payments remain disabled; real sandbox delivery, account eligibility, funding availability and production operational validation remain required. Keep this plan uncommitted, following the user's instruction about plans.

## 1. Agreed product model

Use **select and approve while open → charge the approved current basket at group closure → grocery payout before shopping → organizer reward payout after completion**. The reward is `W = max($50, 10% × D)`, where `D` is the paying members' original grocery cost before POOLD markup and service fee. No grocery-payment hold or charge is created while the group is open. A member selects groceries, reviews the server-calculated itemized total and closing date/time, saves a payment method for later use, and clicks **“Approve $X to be charged when the group closes.”** This is permission for a future payment, not a bank authorization reserving funds.

Members can change their items and quantities any time before closure. Every saved basket change invalidates the previous approval, even if the new total is lower or unchanged. Show the new total and require the member to click the approval button again. Approval covers one exact basket revision, total, currency, pricing policy, payment method and closing schedule; it is not blanket permission for future edits. Show **“Changes need approval”** until the latest basket is approved. Removing all items or leaving revokes the scheduled charge.

At the scheduled deadline, freeze the group's current baskets and stop ordinary edits. Charge only a basket with valid current approval, and only at or after that actual closure. Never charge the previously approved basket after the member has changed it. If a final price, fee or schedule differs from what a member approved, obtain fresh approval before charging. Do not silently increase another member's bill when someone leaves or fails to pay. If a buyer cannot pay, follow section 2's retry and voluntary takeover process; the organizer may also cancel. Preserve unaffected buyers' bills and require approval of any additional purchase. After every required payment in the final approved closure/recovery revision succeeds and any organizer-owned replacement shares are explicitly covered, transfer only the member-funded grocery budget through Connect and request the grocery Instant Payout. Shopping starts only when the organizer can access that entire purchasing budget. Keep the collected reward `W` and its payout-cost budget allocated in POOLD until the organizer completes the trip and all required member handouts are confirmed; then pay the organizer the full `W` separately. The approved markup and service-fee revenue together fund the reward and both payouts; members are not charged again when the reward is paid. There is no separate car allowance.

Here, **group closure means the membership/claim cutoff before shopping** (`open → locked`), not completed pickup. The amount known then is the finalized planned purchase. Store-price differences discovered during shopping still require reconciliation; they cannot be known at this earlier closure event.

Keep the existing group order experience: no quick splits, and product suggestions remain inside their group. Start with Canadian organizers, CAD, saved cards and explicit consent for a one-time off-session charge at closure. Saving a card does not guarantee funds or later payment success. Instant Payout is the intended grocery funding method. Budget a separate completion-reward payout using its configured method; the illustrative policy uses Instant Payout for both legs. A standard payout is only an explicitly accepted delay/reschedule fallback, never an automatic replacement for the requested instant flow. Do not introduce wallets, subscriptions, tips, cross-border transfers, or automatic off-session top-ups in this release. Display goods at the 15% marked-up price, the 5% service fee and applicable taxes before approval so there is no undisclosed later debit. The organizer buys their own groceries outside the app: exclude those groceries from member charges, `D`, the grocery payout and the reward calculation. A five-person group including its organizer therefore has four paying members, not five payments.

The user's current pricing directions are:

- Apply a 15% goods markup, followed by a 5% service fee on the marked-up subtotal. Before additional taxes and rounding, members pay `D × 1.15 × 1.05 = 1.2075D`, a 20.75% uplift. Both markup and service-fee revenue fund the reward and provider costs; do not add an extra processing surcharge.
- One reward of **$50 or 10% of `D`, whichever is higher**, per completed run, with travel included. Freeze the reward on the approved closure snapshot (or an explicitly confirmed pre-funding recovery revision under section 2), retain its funding in POOLD until the trip and required handouts are complete, and release it once as a separate payout. Budget both payout costs within the existing uplift. No separate mileage/car allowance.
- Use comparisons to report honest all-in savings, not to set the fee. Prefer at least 10% verified basket savings in the pilot; disclose missing evidence or a higher cost clearly. Enforce any savings guarantee explicitly accepted for a particular group.
- Allocate markup and service-fee cents proportionately under section 3; model the actual number of member card payments for viability. Greater reference prices or provider costs do not justify raising the approved 15%/5% rates.
- Exclude operating costs, the illustrative $10 allowance and the monthly connected-account fee from current order pricing and break-even calculations. This is a scoped payment-cost calculation, not a statement that POOLD has no other expenses.
- Two planned payouts per completed group: grocery funds after closure/all required payments, and reward `W` after verified trip/handout completion. Each requires actual account/funds eligibility and its own idempotent operation. Every member approving early never triggers charging or payout.
- No hidden grocery contingency or fee buffer. Members approve the displayed current total without reserving funds; changes require fresh approval. Do not add an unseen charge ceiling.
- POOLD funds provider-fee losses, pricing variance, refunds and operational reserves from its own capital. Post-closure funding removes the organizer's grocery advance; it does not remove POOLD's need for capital.

## 2. Decisions required before live payments

### Business model and fee classification

Ask Stripe to confirm that POOLD's actual grocery purchasing and organizer relationship is supported, who acts as merchant, and which Canadian connected account configuration is appropriate. Stripe prohibits peer-to-peer money transmission; describing a reimbursement flow as a marketplace is insufficient. This validation is a launch dependency, not an assumption that every student can receive marketplace payouts. [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)

The earlier flat $2.50/$5.05 fees and 20% markup were illustrations, not approved universal prices. The new model is commercial pricing for the grocery/group-order service, including organizer compensation. Establish whether POOLD sells goods or arranges a purchase as agent, and how the markup must be represented. A commercial margin is not automatically a card surcharge; conversely renaming a card surcharge does not change its classification. Canadian credit-card surcharge rules include a 2.4% cap and other requirements; service/convenience fees have separate eligibility rules. Confirm the actual model and disclosures before launch rather than treating any label as an exemption. [FCAC fee rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/merchants/credit-fees-merchant.html)

Determine registration, agency/merchant treatment, and tax obligations before selecting a tax rule. A genuine platform service fee must not automatically inherit the tax treatment of a credit-card surcharge. Grocery funding includes applicable store taxes; avoid adding those taxes again. Record any tax on POOLD's fee separately. [CRA guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-200/application-gst-hst-credit-card-surcharges.html)

### Provider costs and funding feasibility

Public Canadian prices checked for the November 5 planning discussion provide inputs: domestic online cards at 2.9% + CA$0.30; platform-managed Connect pricing lists CA$2 per monthly active account and 0.25% + CA$0.25 per payout, with Instant Payouts at 1%. The Connect comparison also lists a 0.25% funds-routing charge. The examples model all three percentage payout components together, or 1.5% + CA$0.25 per payout, subject to confirmation of applicability and stacking. Exclude the monthly account fee from the current order/break-even calculation as directed by the user. Confirm the actual agreement, all minimums and extras before configuring a live policy. [Payments pricing](https://stripe.com/en-ca/pricing), [Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/en-ca/connect/pricing)

Instant Payouts require account, destination, balance and platform-limit eligibility. Canadian destinations use eligible debit cards. A successful payment does not prove our chosen transfer flow can fund an instant payout immediately. Validate the exact charge → transfer → payout path with Stripe, including first payouts and pending funds; never promise an instant arrival time. [Instant Payouts documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/instant-payouts)

### Scheduled charge consent and payment failure

Use a Stripe SetupIntent (or hosted Checkout in setup mode) to save the payment method for future off-session use without collecting the grocery payment. Persist the member's explicit agreement to one charge for this basket at the displayed group closing date/time, including how the total is determined. Record successful provider setup separately from the member's basket approval; neither proves a later charge will succeed. Banks can still require card validation/authentication during setup. [Stripe Setup Intents and consent](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/setup-intents)

At actual closure, create and confirm an automatically captured PaymentIntent for the frozen approved total using the saved Customer/payment method and `off_session=true`. Do not create a manual-capture grocery PaymentIntent or reserve the basket amount while open. A successful setup cannot be treated as paid or funded. [Save a payment method and charge later](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/save-and-reuse.md?payment-ui=elements)

A charge can be declined for insufficient funds, an expired card or another issuer decision, or require the member to return for bank authentication. Show **“Payment action required”**, notify the member and recover against the same frozen basket within a bounded payment-recovery window. Keep payout and shopping blocked until the whole batch succeeds. If the buyer still cannot pay, offer voluntary share takeover to the group under the process below. The organizer may cancel instead; cancel and refund if neither retry nor takeover completes within the configured deadlines. On cancellation, refund successful payments and cancel unfinished payment attempts where possible. There is no atomic multi-card transaction and no reserved money guaranteeing payment. [Later authentication and recovery](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/setup-intents#specify-usage-to-increase-success-rate)

An approval has no card-hold expiry. Its validity instead depends on the latest basket revision, itemized total, fee policy, payment method, closing date/time and any disclosed quote-validity terms. A changed closing date/time requires renewed consent. An organizer cannot charge members earlier than the schedule they approved: an early-close action requires approval of the new charge timing from every affected member; otherwise keep the original payment schedule. A late worker runs the closure charge only after the recorded cutoff and revalidates the approval before submitting it.

### Buyer retry, group notification and voluntary takeover

After a closure-time payment failure, use **buyer retry → notify the whole group and offer voluntary takeover → proceed fully funded or organizer cancellation**. The group remains closed to ordinary edits, and grocery payout and shopping stay blocked throughout recovery.

1. **Buyer retry first.** Notify the affected buyer privately, show their exact unpaid items/total and the retry deadline, and let them update their saved payment method or complete bank authentication. Reconcile/reuse the existing payment attempt for the unchanged instruction; a method change requires fresh approval. Do not create duplicate charges or retry indefinitely. Retry limits and both recovery deadlines must be configured and disclosed before launch.
2. **Notify every group member when takeover opens.** If the buyer still cannot pay after the retry limit/window, create a durable in-app notification for **all current group members, including the organizer and affected buyer**, and send push notifications through the existing notification preferences/delivery system. Explain that shares are available, identify items and quantities, show the takeover deadline and link directly to the group's available-shares screen. Keep decline reasons and card details private. Persist the notification event with recovery-round/recipient deduplication keys so worker retries do not repeatedly alert everyone; delivery failure does not remove the in-app offer. Notify the group when recovery succeeds or the run is canceled, and notify affected buyers of completed reassignments.
3. **Offer voluntary claims.** Other members may claim some or all available shares. Show item quantities, original grocery cost, 15% markup, subsequent 5% service fee, tax and the exact additional payment before confirmation. An already-paid member approves only the new purchase, charged separately under a recovery instruction; their existing purchase is neither charged again nor silently repriced. Nobody is assigned shares or a higher bill automatically. Use short, expiring claim reservations and transactional quantity checks so concurrent claims cannot sell the same share twice. A reservation with a processing or unknown payment stays unavailable until reconciled; expiry alone never reallocates already-paid shares.
4. **Stop the original liability before reassignment.** Reconcile unknown/processing provider results before offering the corresponding shares. End or cancel the failed buyer's superseded payment attempt where supported, revoke the superseded approval and prevent further retries for transferred shares. A buyer retry and takeover confirmation must serialize against the same quantities; a successful original payment prevents their reassignment. If part of a basket is transferred, the original buyer needs a new exact approval/instruction to pay for anything retained. Unexpected late charges are reconciled and refunded where invalid, never treated as a second owner/payment for the shares.
5. **Allow organizer coverage or cancellation.** The organizer can voluntarily take remaining shares as their own purchase, confirming the exact quantity and out-of-pocket store cost. Organizer-owned takeovers are excluded from app charges, member-funded `D`, grocery payout and reward basis. This is payment for their own goods, not an advance for other members. The organizer can also cancel the run during recovery; close offers, stop further charging, reconcile in-flight results and refund all successful run payments, including successful takeover payments, under section 5.
6. **Finalize the revised run before grocery funding.** Append an immutable recovery revision linked to the original closure snapshot; retain ownership, approval, payment and cancellation history. Member-to-member takeover of the same goods preserves `D` and `W`. Organizer takeover reduces member-funded `D`: recalculate `W = max($50, 10% × revised D)`, show the revised grocery funding and reward, and require organizer confirmation before finalizing. Existing buyers' paid baskets and amounts stay unchanged; retain their original component allocations and record any recovery rounding adjustment explicitly at POOLD's expense. This disclosed pre-funding revision is the only takeover-related reward recalculation; later shopping shortfalls retain the final frozen reward under section 5.
7. **Proceed only when complete and viable.** Every required share must have a confirmed paying owner or explicit organizer-owned coverage; partial takeover cannot leave an unfunded pack remainder. Recheck formation terms, reward funding, both payout budgets and actual payment costs, including additional takeover transactions and retained costs from failed attempts. No extra processing surcharge, forced purchase, automatic fee increase or silent reward reduction makes an underfunded run viable. If every condition passes, supersede unpaid instructions and proceed from the final revision without recharging successful purchases. If shares remain uncovered or the revised run is not viable, the organizer may cancel earlier; otherwise cancel/refund when the takeover deadline expires. No grocery payout or shopping may begin with unresolved ownership, payment or funding.

These recovery revisions do not reopen ordinary basket editing or authorize arbitrary post-closure purchases. They are explicit replacements for unpaid shares in the closed run. Organizer cancellation and late-payment reconciliation must also stop outstanding takeover reservations and jobs safely.

## 3. Pricing: 15% goods markup, 5% service fee and a $50-or-10% reward

### Pricing rule and reward basis

Apply **15% markup to the paying members' original store cost**, then **5% service fee to the marked-up subtotal**. The 5% fee is not applied to original cost alone. Before additional taxes and rounding:

```text
D = paying members' original grocery cost
M = 0.15D
F = 0.05(D + M) = 0.0575D
T = D + M + F = 1.2075D
W = max($50, 0.10D)
```

The organizer earns one net completion reward `W`: $50 for grocery budgets up to and including $500, then 10% for larger budgets. This is 10% of original grocery cost, not the marked-up goods price or the final member payment. Organizer-owned groceries purchased outside the app are excluded from `D`, the payout and reward basis. For a five-person group including the organizer, there are four card payments. Other group sizes use their actual number of nonzero paying members.

This supersedes the old fixed $50 reward, the variable cost-recovery service-fee solver and the illustrative $10 operating allowance. Markup and service-fee revenue both fund organizer compensation and provider costs; the 5% fee is not the sole revenue source. Do not add a processing surcharge or increase the 15%/5% rates to make an underfunded group viable. Retail comparisons measure member value and do not set prices. The commercial receipt/tax classification still needs section 2 validation.

### Authoritative inputs

Use bounded integer cents for:

- `G_i`: paying member i's original purchasing subtotal, including applicable store taxes and exact funded pack/remainder allocations. Phase 0 must confirm taxable bases and receipt presentation; never duplicate store taxes. The numerical examples have no additional tax.
- `D = sum(G_i)`: grocery budget funded by the paying members. Every planned pack and remainder needs a payer; the organizer's separately purchased share is excluded.
- `M`, `F`, `T`: goods markup, service fee and total member charges, with any additional tax recorded separately.
- `W = max(5000, round_half_up(D × 1000 / 10000))`: reward in cents, calculated on the frozen approved closure budget or final explicitly confirmed pre-funding recovery revision under section 2, with travel included and no car allowance. Store the basis, policy version and reward amount on the closure snapshot.
- `n`: actual number of nonzero member payment instructions in the quoted batch; exclude the organizer and zero-purchase members. Model these before charging, not only after success. Count actual provider transactions separately for voluntary takeover charges and any exceptional supplemental payment; a member already charged may have an additional takeover transaction.
- `C = sum(card_cost(X_i))`: modeled card processing on each member's full payment, including markup, service fee and any applicable additional tax.
- `Q_g(D)` and `Q_w(W)`: modeled costs of the grocery and later completion-reward payouts, including applicable percentage, fixed, instant, routing and minimum charges under the confirmed contract.
- `Q_total = Q_g(D) + Q_w(W)`: round each payout separately and include two fixed payout charges. Budget for the organizer to receive net `D` before shopping and net `W` after completion.
- `R = sum(X_i) - additional_taxes - D - W - C - Q_g(D) - Q_w(W)`: POOLD's retained amount before excluded operating/monthly account costs. Payment-cost break-even means `R = 0`, not zero profit after every business expense.

Do not add operating allowance, monthly connected-account recovery or an extra profit target to this pricing formula or the current break-even examples. Those actual expenses remain separate accounting records. A manual organizer price is an estimate; record its source and verify it against store evidence. Because the percentage reward scales with `D`, fabricated or inflated store costs must never increase compensation. Reconciliation returns unspent member grocery money under section 5.

### Deterministic quotes and viability calculation

1. For the current basket, and again at closure, resolve funded quantities and exact `G_i` allocations. Reject a zero-budget group.
2. Calculate the group markup `M = round_half_up(D × 1500 / 10000)`. Allocate `M_i` proportionately to `G_i` with largest remainder, using stable member-ID ties.
3. Calculate `F = round_half_up((D + M) × 500 / 10000)`. Allocate `F_i` proportionately to `G_i + M_i`, again with largest remainder and stable ties. Then `X_i = G_i + M_i + F_i + additional_tax_i`. The legal receipt may present marked-up goods prices; preserve the original cost/markup allocation internally for exact refunds. Freeze original line-level allocations too.
4. Calculate the once-per-group `W`, each member's card cost and both payout costs using the configured provider rounding/minimum rules. Verify `R >= 0` for payment-cost viability. These costs are funded by `M + F`; there is no fee solver or separately charged per-member processing amount.
5. If the group cannot fund net `D`, net `W` and modeled payment/payout costs at the fixed rates, block charging and seek more funded purchases or cancel under the published formation terms. Do not silently lower `W`, raise the fee, move organizer-owned costs into the member budget or charge an unapproved basket. Any exceptional POOLD subsidy must be explicit and funded, not an automatic pricing assumption.
6. Require current approval of every exact final member total, tax, policy and schedule before charging. Persist the frozen reward basis, amount, member/line allocations, provider assumptions, both payout budgets and viability result under an immutable policy version. Reconcile actual provider fees separately and absorb unquoted differences without a later customer debit.

Round quoted money deterministically in cents, with half-up rounding for commercial percentages and largest remainder for shared allocations. Use provider-specific rounding for actual fee estimates; do not round once on the aggregate card total when there are multiple charges. The simplified calculations below are rounded aggregate planning estimates, not exact per-transaction settlement promises.

### Formation minimum and basket approval

Set formation terms before publication and apply the actual modeled viability check to the final group. No final fixed product minimum is chosen here. The approximately $333.05 break-even below assumes four paying members and the stated fee model; it is not a universal minimum for every group size, tax rule, card type or payout configuration. It supersedes the earlier proposed $400/two-payer setting.

While open, show marked-up goods, the separate 5% service fee, additional applicable tax, complete total and closing schedule. The button approves the displayed amount without a hold. Store an immutable quote/approval. Every saved basket edit invalidates that member's prior approval, including reductions and same-total substitutions. Require fresh approval if any other member's quoted amount or terms change, including allocation-rounding differences. Never use comparison prices to inflate a quote.

At closure, freeze the final roster and baskets; recompute `D`, `W`, charges and viability. Charge only when the group meets its formation terms, every required member has approved their exact current terms, and the reward and both payout costs are funded. Missing approval blocks all initial charges. Retry approval covers the same frozen instruction. A voluntary takeover requires its own exact approval and an immutable recovery revision under section 2; it cannot reuse the failed buyer's consent. Do not fall back to an earlier basket or silently remove a payer. All members approving early never triggers payment or payout.

### Retail comparisons and savings claims

Maintain location/channel-specific reference observations with exact product/quantity/grade matching, source, timestamp, availability and promotion expiry. Keep warehouse, pickup, online and delivery prices separate. Source prices are not stock guarantees; acquisition promotions must apply at purchase time.

- Show verified all-in savings as `reference basket - final customer total`, including both markup and service fee.
- Prefer at least 10% verified basket savings in the pilot. This is a product target, not a promise that every imported product is cheaper.
- Show comparison coverage; do not extrapolate partial item matches to a whole-basket saving. With no evidence, say **Savings not verified**.
- When a direct-purchase comparison is cheaper than POOLD's full price, disclose that before approval. Enforce any explicitly promised savings guarantee or revise/block before charging.
- A comparison-price change cannot alter the 15%/5% pricing formula. It may change a savings label or a promised-deal eligibility check.

The earlier half-savings allocation model is superseded. Honest comparisons, accepted prices, valid funding and the full calculated reward remain required.

### Member receipt and settlement

Before approval, show selected goods at the 15% marked-up price, the marked-up subtotal, **5% service fee**, applicable additional tax, complete total and closing date/time. Explain the markup and fee basis transparently. Use **“Approve $X to be charged when the group closes”**, followed by **“Approved — scheduled for [date/time]”**. Explain that no basket funds are reserved and payment may require action at closure. After an edit, show **“Changes need approval”**. Describe the organizer reward as **“$50 or 10% of members' original grocery cost, whichever is higher, paid after completion.”** Markup and service-fee revenue fund it and the payment/payout fees together; do not call the service fee an exact Stripe charge or add another charge later. Final legal receipt presentation follows the merchant/tax decision.

Track original store cost, original line allocations, markup, service fee, tax, reward funding, approved total/schedule, paid amounts and refunds separately. At closure charge the current approved total once. The grocery payout delivers only `D`. Keep `W` and the reward-payout budget allocated in POOLD until verified completion, then deliver net `W` separately. Show grocery funding, reward pending, reward eligible, payout pending and reward paid distinctly.

If store changes reduce actual purchases after charging, refund unspent grocery money and the corresponding original markup/service-fee/tax allocations under section 5. Preserve the closure-frozen reward for an otherwise valid completed run, following the existing completion/refund rule; do not lower earned compensation or raise remaining member bills to recover a shortfall. No automatic increase in reward follows a supplemental purchase. POOLD's reserve covers unquoted gaps; full cancellation and recovery follow section 5.

### Illustrative calculations and break-even

Assumptions: CAD; four paying members in a five-person group including the organizer; domestic cards at 2.9% + $0.30 per payment; two Instant Payouts, each modeled at 0.25% payout + 0.25% routing + 1% instant, plus $0.25 per payout. Thus the planning payout cost is `Q(P) = 0.015P + $0.25`, subject to confirmation of actual stacking, minimums and rounding. No additional taxes, operating costs, $10 allowance or monthly account fee are included. See section 2 for provider sources. For different group sizes, use actual `n`, not four by default.

Ignoring transaction-level rounding, the model is:

```text
T = 1.2075D
W = max(50, 0.10D)
C = 0.029T + 0.30n
Q_total = 0.015(D + W) + 0.50  # two payouts, two fixed fees
R = T - D - W - C - Q_total
  = 0.1574825D - 1.015W - 0.30n - 0.50
```

For `n = 4` and `D <= $500`, `W = $50`, so `R = 0.1574825D - 52.45`. Setting `R = 0` gives **approximately $333.05 original grocery cost**, about $402.16 collected from members. It pays the organizer $50 and leaves POOLD approximately $0 after the modeled card and payout fees. This covers the organizer reward and Stripe fees; it excludes the requested operating/monthly account costs. Use the exact-cent feasibility check before charging: rounding, taxes and different payment counts can change the practical threshold.

For `D > $500`, `W = 0.10D`, so with four payers `R = 0.0559825D - 1.70`. The reward increases with basket size; it is not capped at $50.

## 4. Order and payment workflow

1. **Organizer setup.** Complete Stripe-hosted Connect onboarding and verify required capabilities, instant destination and payout controls before members approve. Store provider IDs and readiness summaries, not raw bank/card information.
2. **Select, review and approve while open.** Calculate an itemized server quote. Save the member's card for future off-session use through Stripe-hosted setup if needed, verify setup on the server, and record their explicit approval for the quoted basket, amount and closing schedule. No grocery-payment hold, charge, cash, fee revenue, transfer or payout is posted. All members approving is a readiness signal only.
3. **Edit and approve again.** Accept item/quantity edits only while open. Atomically mark the existing approval stale when an edit is saved; calculate and display the new total and require another click. This includes decreases and same-total substitutions. Update fee-dependent quotes for other affected members without changing their approved amounts silently. Leaving or emptying the basket revokes consent for this group. A card need not be set up again for each basket edit if the saved method is still valid; approval of the new basket is still required. A late setup completion cannot approve a different or newer basket.
4. **Close the group exactly once.** At the approved deadline, atomically move `open → locked`, stop claim edits and snapshot the final roster/baskets and approvals. Do not equate closure with everyone approving or a setup webhook. Cancel unfinished setup sessions where supported; reconcile late events without creating consent or charges. Require every affected member's approval before an early closure changes the charge schedule.
5. **Resolve actual purchases and service fees before charging.** Apply approved full-pack/shortfall decisions. The organizer funds their own shares and any leftovers they accept outside the app; exclude those amounts from member charges, `D`, the grocery payout and the reward basis. Otherwise skip/reduce unfunded purchases and obtain approval of changed member baskets/totals. Every member-funded pack allocation must have a payer. Allocate exact pack costs using largest remainder arithmetic. Apply section 3's server-owned 15% markup and subsequent 5% service fee; require funding for net grocery budget `D`, reward `W = max($50, 10% × D)` and both payout costs. Exclude operating and monthly account costs from this viability check. Validate savings claims and any explicitly promised guarantee independently. Freeze original grocery/markup/service-fee/tax amounts, the reward basis and amount, comparisons, consent references and payment instructions.
6. **Preflight the whole batch.** Check each current approval against the frozen basket, exact component/total amounts, policy, saved payment method and charge schedule. Skip zero-purchase members without creating a payment. Missing or stale approval blocks the batch before any charges begin. Seek fresh approval against the frozen basket within a bounded recovery window, or cancel. A valid saved card establishes readiness to attempt payment, not available funds.
7. **Charge individual final amounts into POOLD; recover unpaid shares if needed.** Only a closed, preflighted batch may create and confirm automatically captured off-session PaymentIntents. Use one stable business-operation key and one PaymentIntent per member's frozen instruction, and reconcile/reuse that attempt during retries. These remain separate card transactions even though they belong to one group. Confirm every payment; there is no atomic multi-card charge. If a charge is declined or needs authentication, keep payout blocked, reconcile unknown results and request member action against the same PaymentIntent within the bounded recovery window. If buyer retries fail, notify all group members and offer voluntary takeover under section 2, preserving successful purchases and requiring new approval for additional shares. The organizer may take remaining shares as their own purchase or cancel the run. Finalize a fully funded, viable recovery revision before payout; if recovery cannot complete by its deadline or the organizer cancels, refund successful original and takeover charges and cancel unfinished attempts where possible. POOLD absorbs retained provider costs.
8. **Pay out grocery funds only.** After every required payment in the final active closure/recovery revision succeeds, every organizer-owned share is explicitly covered and viability is confirmed, transfer the grocery portions totaling `D` to the organizer's connected account and request one grocery Instant Payout. Source-linked transfers may be separate per charge; they feed this grocery payout. Retain reward `W`, its future payout-cost budget and tax liabilities in POOLD; track remaining markup/service-fee revenue separately. Verify actual funds, limits and net receipt: the organizer must receive the full `D` for shopping. Do not transfer/release the reward early or let grocery refunds/retries consume its allocated balance. No car allowance, partial grocery-budget payout or payout from approvals alone. Track requested/pending/paid/failed states distinctly.

9. **Start shopping when funded.** Require accessible funds and organizer acknowledgment. Instant-ineligible or unavailable funds produce an explicit wait/reschedule or cancellation path; standard fallback needs acceptance and a refund of any unused instant-specific fee portion. A successful payment/transfer is insufficient proof that grocery money is accessible. Never require personal spending.
10. **Reconcile shopping and pickup.** Store-price reductions/unavailable items produce refunds against successful payments. Higher prices require skipping/reducing or an explicitly approved supplemental payment, funded before the additional purchase. Initial basket consent never permits an automatic top-up. Any supplemental payout is separately approved and tracked; it is outside the two planned grocery/reward payouts. For the pilot POOLD absorbs extra processing/payout costs from approved price-change supplements. Record the store receipt and final exact-cent allocations. Pickup uses the existing collection codes; its status does not erase pending refunds/disputes.
11. **Release the calculated reward after completion.** Require evidence the trip is complete, the receipt/items are reconciled, and all required member handouts are confirmed before the reward becomes eligible. An organizer marking the trip done alone is insufficient. A missed pickup does not automatically count as handed out; unresolved bags need the published no-show/support process before the handout obligation can be closed. Then transfer the already collected reward allocation and issue one separately idempotent payout delivering net `W` from the frozen closure snapshot. Charge no member again and deduct no payout fee from the promised reward. Payout failure leaves the reward owed/pending for safe retry and never reopens customer charging. An unresolved completion/refund dispute routes to support; any payout pause must follow disclosed terms.

Payout timing depends on real availability, not just the group deadline. Separate transfers linked to successful charges can remain pending until source funds become available. Validate that the exact Canadian Connect flow can support immediate group payout, rather than assuming a successful charge creates instant organizer liquidity. If a trip is too soon for the supported path, the app must prevent an unfunded shopping commitment. [Transfer availability](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers.md?platform=web&integration=checkout&ui=stripe-hosted)

## 5. Refunds, cancellations and risk

Recommended customer policy, disclosed before payment:

| Situation | Customer outcome | POOLD/organizer accounting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card setup/approval abandoned, member leaves, or group cancels before charging | No grocery charge; revoke scheduled consent and cancel unneeded pending attempts | Reconcile late setup/payment events; show canceled, not refunded when no payment succeeded |
| Basket changes before closure, including reductions | Show the revised total and require another approval; the old basket cannot be charged | Mark previous approval stale atomically; no hold or release exists |
| Final fee/total or closing schedule changes, or latest approval is missing | Request approval of the current frozen terms before charging, or cancel | No silent price/timing change, stale-basket charge or charge before closure |
| Saved card fails or the bank requires authentication at closure | Offer buyer retries within the bounded window; if they still cannot pay, notify every group member and open voluntary takeover | Keep payout/shopping blocked; reconcile old attempts before offering shares and require exact approval for takeover payments |
| Voluntary takeover covers some or all unpaid shares | Charge only newly approved member takeovers; organizer may cover shares as their own store purchase | Preserve successful purchases, append recovery revisions and recheck packs, viability and any explicitly confirmed reward change |
| Retry/takeover cannot fully fund a viable run by its deadline, organizer cancels during recovery, or the run is canceled after charging | Refund all successful original and takeover charges, including fees and relevant tax; stop offers and cancel unfinished attempts where possible | Reconcile in-flight charges/refunds; POOLD absorbs retained provider costs; recover any advance already sent |
| Item unavailable or actual price lower | Refund unspent grocery amount and the corresponding original markup, service fee and applicable tax | Never increase the accepted fee on remaining goods; cap total refunds at original payment |
| Higher price | Explicit new checkout or skip/reduce item | No automatic debit or redistribution to other buyers |
| Grocery payout fails | Wait/reschedule, or cancel under the disclosed policy | Retry safely; keep the reward allocated and unpaid; POOLD absorbs unquoted retry costs |
| Reward payout fails after a valid completed run | No additional customer charge | Keep the frozen reward `W` payable to the organizer; retry the same reward operation safely and absorb unquoted costs |
| Buyer misses pickup after valid purchase | Apply published pickup/cancellation terms and support review | No automatic reversal of already incurred grocery costs |
| Dispute or suspected misuse | Support investigation and appropriate account restrictions | Keep evidence, reconcile liabilities and replenish reserves |

For partial refunds, compute cumulative entitlement from the original line receipt and its allocated markup, service fee and tax, then subtract refunds already issued. Refund the relevant unspent grocery amount and proportional original markup/service fee; never reprice fulfilled items upward to replace lost margin. This prevents repeated rounding from over-refunding or retaining extra cents. Tax refunds follow the frozen tax rule. Lower-price refunds must not depend on organizer reimbursement arriving first if the customer policy promises them earlier.

The reward `W = max($50, 10% × D)` is calculated at closure, or on the final explicitly confirmed pre-funding recovery revision under section 2, and remains allocated in POOLD until the trip and required handouts are complete. It is not a compensation advance. A completed otherwise valid run still earns the full closure-frozen `W` when some items were unavailable; do not recalculate the percentage against a smaller post-shopping basket. Fund resulting markup/service-fee/refund gaps from POOLD's reserve without reducing the reward or raising other members' bills. If the whole run is canceled before completion, do not pay the unearned reward: include its customer-funded allocation in the published cancellation/refund calculation, recover unused grocery advances, and absorb nonrecoverable provider costs under the agreed policy. Cancellation after meaningful work requires a published compensation/support policy consistent with the agreed customer refund terms. Record reward funding allocation, unearned amount, earned/payable amount, payout state and any exceptional recovery separately. Travel is included in `W`, so there is no additional travel reimbursement claim.

Stripe does not generally return original processing fees; refunds need available platform funds. Separate-charge refunds do not automatically undo transfers, and recovery after an organizer's bank payout is not guaranteed. [Refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds), [Separate charges and transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers.md?platform=web&integration=checkout&ui=stripe-hosted)

Before live launch, set a funded reserve and conservative group/organizer advance limits. For the initial pilot, reserve enough POOLD capital to refund all outstanding grocery advances plus expected retained provider costs, without using other customers' grocery liabilities. Track unused organizer advances as receivables; obtain an approved recovery agreement/process, reconcile returns and reversals, and block new advances when recovery is overdue. Never assume a bank debit will succeed. Do not describe platform balances or shopping advances as escrow.

## 6. Stripe integration design

Provisional choice: saved payment methods plus closure-time platform payments with **separate charges and transfers**, Stripe-hosted Checkout in `mode=setup`, and hosted Connect onboarding. Configure setup for future off-session use and retain Stripe Customer/payment-method/SetupIntent IDs. Collect basket-specific scheduled-payment consent in POOLD; setup completion alone is not approval of a grocery amount. At actual closure, create/confirm a PaymentIntent for the frozen approved total with automatic capture and `off_session=true`. Record success only from authoritative provider state. Do not use `capture_method=manual`, capturable balances, grocery holds or expiry-driven capture. Validate the charge flow and immediate payout behavior in phase 0 before treating this architecture as production-ready. [Setup Intents](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/setup-intents)

Maintain a separate financial lifecycle alongside the existing order lifecycle:

```text
not_started → setting_up_card → approval_needed → approved_waiting_for_close
            → close_finalizing → charging → paid
            → grocery_transferring → grocery_payout_pending → shopping_funded
            → reconciling_and_handouts → reward_eligible
            → reward_payout_pending → reward_paid → settled

Branches: changes_need_approval, payment_action_required, payment_recovery,
          takeover_open, takeover_payment_pending, recovery_finalizing,
          cancelled, needs_attention, refund_pending
```

A saved card can be reused across approvals, but each group/basket revision has its own consent. Editing an approved basket transitions it to `changes_need_approval`; only a fresh review and click can restore approval. Keep setup, approval, payment, grocery transfer/payout, completion reward and refund states independent. Fulfillment may be complete while its earned reward payout is still pending; that pending financial obligation remains visible. A group stays open even if every member approves early. Existing deadline locking must enqueue close finalization exactly once. The order remains `locked` through charging, buyer retries, voluntary takeover, recovery finalization and payout; `locked → shopping` requires the funding gate. Refunds can remain pending after pickup/completion. Never downgrade a successful payment based on a stale setup event or reset fulfillment because a financial retry failed.

**Migration from the merged implementation:** the November 6 implementation replaces the demo hold-at-close/charge-at-shopping flow and manual-hold Stripe foundation with one shared setup/approval/closure-charge path in test mode. Migrations 012–015 add separate scheduled-payment records and preserve the legacy tables. Validate real sandbox behavior before any production decision. Existing hold/quote/capture tables and provider objects need an explicit migration and reconciliation strategy; do not rename already-applied migrations or treat old holds as new approval. Cancel/reconcile any outstanding sandbox holds separately. Keep production money movement disabled throughout this transition.

### Persistence

Introduce a new financial migration after checking the current migration sequence. Suggested entities:

- `fee_policies`: immutable version, effective date, 15% goods markup, subsequent 5% service fee, approved tax treatment, provider assumptions, separate grocery/reward payout methods/costs, $50 minimum reward and 10% original-cost rate with closure-frozen basis, zero car allowance, explicit exclusion of operating/monthly account costs from current pricing and break-even, formation minimums, fee/guarantee limits, allocation/rounding rules and country/currency scope. New versions affect new consents only.
- Product and price-source records: store/location/channel, product identifiers, exact unit/pack quantity, price/tax observations, source/date/expiry, verification and comparison-match evidence. Existing static catalogue data cannot establish current savings by itself. Preserve source observations and use immutable snapshots in quotes.
- `connected_accounts`: organizer/provider association, capabilities, payout eligibility and synchronization timestamps.
- `payment_methods` / setup references and `basket_approvals`: provider Customer/payment-method/SetupIntent IDs, immutable approved quote/basket revision, exact original grocery/markup/service-fee/tax/total, currency, policy, closing schedule, consent text/version/time, validity/revocation/replacement lineage and optional verified comparisons. Saving a method and approving a basket are distinct operations. No capturable balance or grocery-hold expiry is part of this model.
- `close_batches` and `payment_instructions`: one immutable initial closure snapshot plus linked immutable recovery revisions with one active final revision, final roster/pack allocations, exact member purchasing/markup/service-fee/tax amounts, cost budgets, claim/guarantee checks, reward basis/amount, funding allocations, retained reward-payout budget, approval references, preflight status, recovery deadline and unique payment operations.
- `share_takeovers`: recovery round, available item quantities, original/new owners, expiring reservations, organizer-owned coverage, exact component quotes/approvals, superseded instruction references, transition history and notification event references. Enforce quantity conservation, single ownership and an explicit finalization/cancellation deadline.
- `payment_attempts`: unique PaymentIntent/provider references, requested/paid/refunded amounts, processing/action-required/failure status, actual provider fee references and compensation links. Setup or approval is not a completed purchase.
- `transfers`, `payouts` and allocations: separate `grocery` and `completion_reward` purposes, amounts, source-payment allocations, cost budgets, eligibility evidence and provider/operation references. Grocery funding precedes shopping; reward funding remains allocated in POOLD until completion. Enforce one successful payout per purpose per group, with safe retries; defer combining groups. Compensation is funded/paid once, never once per item/payment/member.
- `refunds` and `organizer_advance_recoveries`: original component allocation, cumulative refund entitlement, provider state and recovery balance.
- `stripe_events` and `payment_operations`: durable deduplicated event inbox and retryable operation outbox, unique business-operation keys and reconciliation state.
- Append-only balanced journal entries: cash, grocery liabilities, purchasing advances, commercial markup/service revenue under the approved accounting model, retained reward funding, organizer compensation expense/payable/paid, tax liabilities, provider expenses, refunds and organizer recoveries. Distinguish compensation from grocery advances and expense from recovery budget. Keep every financial change auditable; statuses alone are not a ledger.

Money uses bounded integer cents, explicit currency, database constraints and stable IDs. Enforce one initial closure snapshot per order revision with append-only recovery lineage and one active revision, one active approval per member/basket version, one successful payment per distinct initial or takeover instruction and one successful grocery payout plus at most one successful completion-reward payout per group, under distinct purpose-specific operation keys. Unknown results must be reconciled before replacements. Approvals and saved payment methods are memorandum records, not cash/fee-revenue postings. Ledger postings are unique per business event and always balance. A connected account balance is shared by that organizer's groups, so internal allocations and controlled payout scheduling must prevent one group's money being consumed twice. Ordinary Stripe balances are not a segregated trust account.

### API and reliability

- Add payment modules under the existing API: fee engine, Stripe client, quotes/checkout, Connect onboarding, reconciliation and financial jobs. Add validated contracts and UI states to the current shared contracts/mobile app.
- Proposed routes cover basket/fee preview, card setup, member approval/revocation/status, organizer close/funding overview, internal close finalization/charging, buyer retry, available takeover shares, reservation/approval, organizer-owned coverage, recovery finalization/cancellation, grocery payout, completion-reward eligibility/payout, receipt reconciliation and role-restricted refunds. Never accept client-supplied prices or another member's quote. Check ownership, basket/quote revision, consent and cutoff server-side. Only closure jobs may initiate the initial scheduled payment; card setup and ordinary basket-approval endpoints cannot charge or trigger payouts. An explicit takeover confirmation may enqueue only its separately approved recovery payment through the durable worker, never recharge the existing basket or start payout directly.
- Mount the signature-verified Stripe webhook using its raw request body **before** the current global JSON parser and browser-origin/session middleware. Limit its payload and authenticate it with Stripe's signature; retain normal authorization on customer APIs. [Webhook guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)
- Verify signatures, persist unique events, then acknowledge. A durable worker processes duplicates and out-of-order delivery safely, retrieves current provider objects when needed, and reconciles missing events. Keep provider API version and webhook version intentional.
- Store operations before external calls. Use stable provider idempotency keys, transactional row locks and unique constraints; never hold a database transaction open across a Stripe network request. A timeout is an unknown result to reconcile, not permission to issue a second payment or payout.
- Match amounts, currency, Customer/payment method, account, quote, approval and business IDs before posting success. A late setup event never approves a newer basket or schedules a charge by itself. An unexpected payment is reconciled and refunded when invalid. Setup events must never be treated as payment success. Use authoritative webhook/retrieval state rather than browser return alone.
- Keep Stripe secret/webhook keys server-only, separate test/live configuration, and redact credentials, checkout URLs and unnecessary personal data from logs. Retain only necessary event information with a retention policy.
- The current sleeping Render Free process and minute timer are insufficient as the sole financial scheduler. Use a durable worker with reliable scheduling and recovery, on hosting suited to the required uptime. Boot-time reconciliation and operator alerts supplement webhook delivery. Confirm hosting costs before live rollout.
- Persist schema/version checks and fail clearly if financial migrations/configuration are missing. Notifications must distinguish card saved, approval needed, approved/scheduled, changes need approval, closing/finalizing, payment action required, shares available to all group members, takeover reserved/paid/reassigned, recovery completed, run canceled, paid, money in transit, shopping funded, scheduled payment canceled, reward awaiting completion, reward eligible, reward payout pending/paid, refund requested and refund completed. Recover closure/payment jobs after sleeping or crashed processes without charging before the approved cutoff or creating duplicate payments.

## 9. Decisions this plan deliberately leaves configurable

Live legal receipt format, merchant/tax treatment, contracted fee components and rounding/minimums, quote-validity, buyer retry limits/windows, takeover deadlines/reservation lifetimes, supported cards, instant funding path, final formation settings, advance limits, reserve size and recovery terms require phase 0 decisions. The 10% verified-savings pilot target remains a proposed product target, not a pricing input or a universal savings claim. Unknown comparisons may not be presented as verified savings.

The user's current directions are fixed:

- **15% goods markup, then a 5% service fee on the marked-up subtotal**, or 20.75% above original cost before additional taxes and rounding. Both are revenue sources; no separate processing surcharge or variable cost-recovery solver.
- **One organizer completion reward of $50 or 10% of the paying members' original grocery cost, whichever is higher**, without a separate car allowance. Freeze the basis and amount at closure or on the explicitly confirmed pre-funding organizer-takeover revision; preserve that final reward for a valid completed run under the existing partial-refund rule.
- Organizer-owned groceries are purchased outside the app and excluded from member charges, the grocery payout and reward basis. A five-person group including the organizer has four paying members.
- Exclude operating costs, the illustrative $10 allowance and the monthly connected-account fee from current pricing/break-even. Approximately $333.05 is the four-payer payment-cost break-even under the illustrative fee assumptions, not a universal approved formation minimum or a statement of net business profit.
- No grocery holds or charges while open; explicit approval of the current basket/total and closing schedule; fresh approval after every basket edit; charge only at/after actual closure.
- Failed buyer gets bounded retries first; if still unable to pay, notify all current group members and offer voluntary takeover with exact new approval. Organizer may take remaining shares outside app payments or cancel; proceed only with complete, viable funding, otherwise cancel/refund.
- Grocery-only payout after all required payments in the final active closure/recovery revision succeed; retain the calculated reward and its payout-cost allocation in POOLD until the trip and required handouts are complete, then pay the full reward separately without another member charge.

Earlier flat-fee, fixed-$50, variable-markup, monthly-overhead and operating-allowance examples are superseded as pricing rules. The demo and Stripe-sandbox code has now been adapted to this design. This does not authorize a live rollout: provider-backed end-to-end tests, operational scheduling, reserves and phase 0 launch decisions remain required.
